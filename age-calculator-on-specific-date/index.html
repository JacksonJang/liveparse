A fixed reference makes the result reproducible

The calculation uses two date-only endpoints

Unlike an age-to-today page, this calculator never substitutes the current date for the endpoint. That makes it useful when reading an old record, describing age at an event, checking a dataset, or planning a future anniversary. Record both input values with the result so another reader can reproduce the same calculation later.

The interval is decomposed by completing whole Gregorian years first, then whole months, then remaining days. A reference exactly equal to the birth date produces zero years, zero months, and zero days. The calculation does not add one merely because a human may call both endpoint dates “included.” Inclusion settings belong to a date-counting problem, not to calendar age.

Date-only arithmetic is intentional. A birthplace, time of birth, event time, and time zone can matter for a highly specific legal or astronomical question, but none is inferred here. The YYYY-MM-DD fields represent civil calendar dates and are not parsed as UTC instants.

Age on a specific date FAQ

What does age on a specific date mean?

It is the completed Gregorian calendar interval from the birth date through the selected reference date, expressed as years, months, and days under this page's stated anniversary rules.

Can the reference date be in the past or future?

Yes. Enter both values as YYYY-MM-DD. The reference date must not be earlier than the birth date, but it may be before or after today because today is not the calculation boundary.

How are February 29 anniversaries treated?

For this calculator, an anniversary of February 29 clamps to February 28 in a non-leap reference year. A legal or organizational rule may choose March 1 instead and must be checked separately.

Does the calculator include the reference date as a whole extra day?

No. Calendar age measures the interval from the start of the birth date to the start of the reference date. It does not add an inclusive extra day merely because both endpoint labels are displayed.

Can this calculation prove age for an official requirement?

No. It is general date arithmetic, not proof of identity or a decision about legal adulthood, work, education, insurance, immigration, retirement, benefits, or any other eligibility rule.
