Calendar units have variable lengths

Why a calendar age is not total days divided by 365

A calendar age answers how many whole anniversaries and calendar-month steps have been completed. The calculation first advances the birth date by whole years without crossing today, then advances by whole months, and finally counts the leftover date boundaries. February can contain 28 or 29 days, while other months contain 30 or 31, so there is no single month length suitable for an exact calendar decomposition.

Total elapsed days can still be useful for a milestone or record, but it answers a different question. Dividing that total by 365 ignores leap days; dividing by 365.2425 gives an astronomical average rather than a birthday convention. This page therefore labels calendar units and elapsed-day totals independently.

Today comes from the browser's current local calendar date. The calculator operates on date-only values rather than midnight timestamps, preventing a time-zone offset from silently moving an entered birthday to the previous day.

Rules and limitations

Age calculator FAQ

How does the age calculator count years, months, and days?

It advances from the birth date through completed Gregorian calendar years, then completed calendar months, then remaining days. It does not divide a total day count by an average year or month length.

What date format should I enter?

Enter the birth date as YYYY-MM-DD using the Gregorian calendar. The labeled fields and native date control may display differently according to browser locale, but the stored value follows that year-month-day order.

How is a February 29 birthday handled?

When an anniversary year has no February 29, this calculator clamps the calendar anniversary to February 28 for its arithmetic. Other institutions may use March 1, so check the rule that governs any official decision.

Is calendar age the same as elapsed time?

No. Calendar age is expressed in completed calendar units whose lengths vary. Total elapsed days is a separate measurement, and elapsed hours can also depend on time zone and daylight-saving transitions.

Can this result determine legal eligibility?

No. The result is general calendar arithmetic and does not decide legal adulthood, employment, school, insurance, retirement, benefit, or identity requirements. Use the responsible authority's definitions and records.
