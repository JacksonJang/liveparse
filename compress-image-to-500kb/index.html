When 500 KB is the right target

What a 500 KB image is good for

A 500 KB limit suits high-detail photos, portfolio galleries, and print-preview images. It is large images where fine texture matters more than the last few kilobytes. Most web-sized photographs reach 500 KB through quality alone, so pixel dimensions are usually preserved.

How the target search works. The compressor encodes the image at decreasing quality levels until the output is under 500 KB, keeping the highest quality that fits. If even the lowest quality is still too large, it scales the dimensions down and tries again, and it tells you when a target was not reached rather than silently returning a bigger file.

Questions about 500 KB images

Compress to 500 KB FAQ

How do I compress an image to 500 KB?

Drop the image on this page: the compressor is preset to a 500 KB target and starts immediately. It searches for the highest quality that still fits under 500 KB, then shows the real encoded size so you can download the result or adjust the format.

Will compressing to 500 KB make my image blurry?

Usually not for typical web sizes. The compressor keeps the highest quality that fits, so a photo that already displays at 1,000 to 2,000 pixels wide normally reaches 500 KB with little visible change. Very large originals may also need smaller pixel dimensions.

Which format reaches 500 KB most easily: JPEG, PNG, or WebP?

WebP and JPEG compress photographs far more efficiently than PNG. PNG stays lossless, so it only gets smaller through fewer pixels or colours; choose it when you need transparency or crisp screenshots. The tool reports whether the 500 KB target was actually reached for each file.

Is my image uploaded to compress it?

No. Decoding, resizing, and re-encoding all happen in your browser tab with the browser's own image encoder. The file never leaves your device, and there is no account or file limit beyond the 25 MB per-file and 20-file batch caps.
