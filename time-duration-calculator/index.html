An earlier clock label needs another fact

Overnight mode places the end on the next calendar day

A clock time alone repeats every day. If the start is 22:30 and the end is 06:15, subtraction cannot tell whether the input is invalid, spans overnight, or refers to unrelated dates. Selecting overnight states that the end belongs to the following day, yielding a nominal gross interval of seven hours and 45 minutes.

When both clock labels are equal, same-day mode represents zero duration. Overnight mode can represent a full 24-hour wall-clock cycle. The distinction is deliberate; the calculator does not guess from an employment context or automatically choose the larger interval.

The break is a duration, not another clock endpoint. It is subtracted once after gross duration is found. Showing gross, break, and net independently prevents a copied result from hiding whether lunch or another pause was already removed.

Wall-clock labels do not identify instants

DST requires a date, time zone, and offset history

A spring-forward night can omit local clock labels, while a fall-back night can repeat them. “01:30 to 03:30” might represent one, two, or three elapsed hours depending on the date and named zone. Because this page accepts only nominal clock times and an overnight choice, it cannot determine which offset transition applies and does not pretend to do so.

Clock-duration answers

Time duration calculator FAQ

How does the calculator handle an end time earlier than the start?

When overnight mode is selected, the end time is interpreted on the following calendar day. Without that selection, an earlier end is invalid rather than silently assumed to cross midnight.

How is a break subtracted?

One nonnegative break duration is subtracted once from the gross wall-clock interval. A break longer than the gross interval is rejected rather than producing a negative worked duration.

Does the calculator account for daylight saving time?

No. Clock times without a date and IANA time zone cannot identify a DST transition. This page performs nominal wall-clock arithmetic; use zoned timestamps and authoritative zone data for elapsed-time work across a transition.

Is 09:00 to 17:00 eight hours if there is a lunch break?

The gross interval is eight hours. With a 30-minute break the net result is seven hours 30 minutes; with a 60-minute break it is seven hours. Enter the break explicitly.

Can this calculator determine payable work time?

No. It supplies arithmetic only. Rounding, paid breaks, overtime, split shifts, on-call time, labor rules, contracts, and payroll policies must be applied by the responsible employer or system.
